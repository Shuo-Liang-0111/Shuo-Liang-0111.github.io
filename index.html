I'm a third-year undergraduate at Xidian University, studying Electronic and Information Engineering, and a remote research intern at the HPC-AI Lab at the National University of Singapore.

Generated video is now good enough that a fabricated clip of a real disaster is cheap to make and hard to dismiss. Most of my time goes into measuring exactly how bad this is — building benchmarks that pit current detectors against current generators, and looking at what happens once those clips start moving through social platforms. More broadly I'm drawn to AIGC, embodied intelligence, and world models: three angles on the same question of how well a model's picture of reality matches the real thing.

How I got here

Mar 2026 — t + 10 mo NUS HPC-AI Lab
remote

I started remote research at NUS in the first week of my fourth semester and have been on one project since: RA-Bench, a benchmark for detecting AI-generated video of real crisis events. Real footage first, generated counterparts second, then a systematic sweep over detectors, generators, and how the clips spread.

It's the first work where I've owned the whole arc — deciding what to measure, running it, and writing it up. That turns out to be a very different skill from doing the experiments someone hands you.

With Yixing Ma (UC Berkeley). Mentored by Pengfei Zhou and Wangbo Zhao, advised by Prof. Yang You. The reason a project this large was open to a sophomore at all is that my mentors brought the collaborators; I'm aware of how much of this was handed to me.
