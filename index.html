I'm a third-year undergraduate at Xidian University, studying Electronic and Information Engineering, and a research intern at the HPC-AI Lab at the National University of Singapore.

Generated video is now good enough that a fabricated clip of a real disaster is cheap to make and hard to dismiss. Most of my time so far has gone into measuring exactly how bad this is — building benchmarks that pit current detectors against current generators, and watching what happens once those clips start moving through social platforms.

What I want to do next is the other half of that problem. Measuring the gap between a generated world and the real one is useful; closing it is more interesting. I'm looking for work on world models — action-conditioned generative models that predict how the physical world evolves, and that an embodied agent can actually plan inside.

How I got here

14–17 Aug 2026 RA-Bench · arXiv

We finished RA-Bench and submitted the manuscript to arXiv on 14 August. It went live on 17 August alongside a 93.8 GB public dataset and evaluation toolkit — and reached #1 on Hugging Face Daily Papers that day.

The thing I keep coming back to from RA-Bench isn't a number. It's that multimodal LLMs and conventional forensic detectors turn out to be looking at different evidence, and the LLMs' mistakes look much more like a human's than like a detector's. Which suggests the two families should be combined rather than ranked against each other.

10 Jul 2026 ACM Multimedia

The decision arrived after rebuttal: DFPR, my first paper, had been accepted to ACM Multimedia 2026. It closed a nine-month loop of early experiments, a rough first draft, several rounds of spring revisions, submission, and rebuttal — much of it while RA-Bench was moving in parallel.

Mar–Apr 2026 DFPR + RA-Bench

When the spring semester began, I revised DFPR through several versions and submitted it to ACM Multimedia. Once the submission was in, I shifted nearly all of my attention to RA-Bench: curating real crisis footage, generating counterparts, building the three evaluation tracks, and running experiments at scale.

Lunar New Year 2026 NUS HPC-AI Lab
remote

Around Lunar New Year, I joined the HPC-AI Lab as a remote research intern and began collaborating on what would become RA-Bench: a benchmark for detecting AI-generated videos of real crisis events, from real footage and generated counterparts to detectors, generators, and social dissemination.

It became the first project where I owned the whole arc — deciding what to measure, implementing the protocol, running every experiment, and writing the paper. The largest single run took twelve days on sixteen A800s; I learned more from the parts that broke than from the results.

With Yixing Ma (UC Berkeley). Mentored by Pengfei Zhou and Wangbo Zhao, and advised by Prof. Yang You. My mentors opened the door to a collaboration of this scale; what I did after that door opened is the work I am proudest of.

Jan 2026 DFPR · first draft

By January, DFPR had a first complete draft. It was rough, but carrying an idea through experimental design, implementation, evaluation, and writing made research feel less like something done by other people and more like a craft I could learn.

2021 – 2024 Qingdao No. 2 High School

Qingdao No. 2 High School helped turn the immature version of me who arrived from junior high into someone steadier and more self-possessed. Papers

Can We Defend Against AI-Generated Video Attacks on Real-World Crisis Events? A Systematic Evaluation of Detectors, Generators and Social Dissemination

Shuo Liang*, Yixing Ma*, Pengfei Zhou*, … , Chunhua Shen, Wei Wang, Yang You, Zheng Zhu, Kaipeng Zhang, Wangbo Zhao

arXiv preprint, 2026co-first author · first of three, 36 authors total

arXiv·code·dataset·project page

🤗Hugging Face Daily Papers#1

RA-Bench pairs 1,830 real crisis videos with 16,056 generated counterparts from nine generators — 17,886 clips, 93.8 GB — and adds two stress tracks: 633 clips that every assigned human reviewer judged real, and a six-stage social-dissemination chain. I designed the three-track evaluation protocol, implemented and ran every experiment, and wrote the paper.

DFPR: Dynamic Fine-Grained Perceptive Bidirectional Image-Text Retrieval

Mengru Ma, Shuo Liang, Wenping Ma, Lingling Li, Xu Liu, Xu Cong

ACM Multimedia 2026first student author

OpenReview

Parameter-efficient fine-tuning for remote-sensing image–text retrieval: mixture-of-experts routing over caption semantics, spectral graph filtering to suppress background clutter, and adaptive reweighting of the two retrieval directions. Best mean recall among PEFT methods on three benchmarks. I designed and ran the experiments and wrote the paper.
